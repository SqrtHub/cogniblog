A response can be clear on paper and hard to read in a photograph. A shadow across a line, a camera focused on the desk, or a cropped margin can hide part of what a student wrote.

Before uploading assessments, open the images and check the handwriting. These steps help make the work readable for a person and prepare files for a grading tool. They do not, on their own, guarantee that every handwritten response can be recognized.

## Keep the whole page in view

Place the assessment on a flat surface and hold your phone directly above it rather than photographing it at an angle. Include the edges of the page and a small margin around them. This makes it easier to spot anything left outside the frame.

Check the ends of responses, question numbers, and notes near the margins. Cutting off a sentence can change its meaning. If there is an answer on the back, photograph that side too.

## Adjust the lighting before moving closer

Find a position where the page is evenly lit. Move your phone or the paper if your hand casts a shadow over the writing. If glare covers part of the page, adjust the angle and check again.

The aim is to keep all pen and pencil marks visible. A filter that makes the background very white can erase faint pencil marks or parts of a formula. Choose a readable image over one that simply looks cleaner.

## Open the image and read a section

A thumbnail in your gallery can hide blur. Open the photo, zoom in on some smaller handwriting, and check whether the handwriting is still clear. Look at symbols, accents, corrections, and any details that matter for that question.

If the photograph is the problem, take it again. If the information is already illegible on paper, a different photo will not recover it. In that case, the difficulty is in the original document.

No megapixel count or camera setting works for every assessment. The most useful check is to examine the file you actually intend to upload.

## Check the page order before uploading

Keep each assessment's pages together and preserve their original order. Before finishing, look for missing, duplicate, or upside-down pages. If the upload method allows you to name files, use a sequence you can recognize without reopening every image.

Use the submission channel appropriate for your school. Avoid sharing assessments that identify students on public pages or in public groups.

## A quick final check

Before uploading, check that:

- you have captured the whole page and any answers on the back;
- the writing is in focus, with no shadows or glare hiding parts of it;
- image processing has not erased any pen or pencil marks;
- the pages are complete and in the right order.
